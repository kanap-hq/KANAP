The first hour with a tool often decides what comes next. 26.11.1 is about that hour. The trial asks what brings you and guides you through five steps, on an example company in your language or on your own data. A record is created by typing its name. And Plaid, the AI assistant built into KANAP, imports your spreadsheets into the right list.

We also went after the sentences nobody needed: explanations move behind an icon, and a refusal says why.

## A trial that starts from what brings you

![At the first opening of a trial workspace, KANAP asks what brings you: the IT budget, the IT landscape map, or projects and requests. The next question is how to start.](/screenshots/blog/trial-first-opening-2026-11-08.png)

**Two questions at the first opening.** Sign-up asks for your first and last name. At the first opening, KANAP asks what brings you (the IT budget, the IT landscape map, projects and requests), then how to start: with the complete example or with your own data. The example, the fictional Fromage & Co, loads in the background in the workspace language: English, French, German or Spanish.

![The home page of a trial workspace puts the section of the chosen path first, with the “Your first five steps” tile.](/screenshots/blog/trial-first-steps-2026-11-08.png)

**Five first steps per path.** The home page puts the section of the chosen path first, with the **Your first five steps** tile. Each step opens its screen with a short hint. The manual has a new [First steps](https://doc.kanap.net/first-steps/budget-example/) section, one short guide per path and mode, and the Help button of a screen opened by a step leads to that step's guide.

**Plaid with one checkbox.** During the trial, an administrator turns Plaid on from its own page, with a single checkbox that names the provider of the included model. The suggested questions follow your path. To talk with us, **Book a discovery call** waits at the end of the first steps and in **About KANAP**.

**A few e-mails, easy to stop.** A welcome with your first steps, a personal note after five days, a reminder two days before the end, a notice at the end. One link stops the next ones in one click. The trial ends at the end of its last day. Without a subscription, the workspace stays readable for thirty days, then it is deleted with all its data.

**A workspace ready to use.** Every new workspace starts with Windows Server and Ubuntu in its technologies, with end of support and vulnerabilities tracked. It also has a portfolio classification, phase templates and evaluation criteria, named in its language.

## Create a record by typing its name

![A new application being created: the name typed in the header, the Create and Cancel buttons, and every tab already available.](/screenshots/blog/create-at-title-2026-11-08.png)

You type the name in the header, and the record exists at once, with all its tabs. Required fields are checked when you create it.

**Everywhere in KANAP.** Applications, requests, projects, incidents, budget lines, contracts, assets, connections, interfaces, tasks, documents, business processes, and the reference data, from companies to sites. An interface also waits for its source and target applications.

**Create or Cancel.** While a record is being created, its header shows two buttons: **Create** keeps it and takes you back where you came from, **Cancel** deletes it. Tab after the title moves to the first field.

## Plaid imports your spreadsheets

![In Plaid, the mapping of an applications spreadsheet: for each import column, the file column read, the confidence, then the first rows.](/screenshots/blog/plaid-import-mapping-2026-11-08.png)

Your applications or contracts often live in a spreadsheet with columns of your own. Plaid puts it into the import's format.

**Attach the file, Plaid maps it.** Plaid accepts an `.xlsx` workbook or a CSV file, up to 5 MB and 20,000 rows. Ask it to import the file into a list. For each column of the import, it shows the column of your file it reads and how sure it is, how it read dates, numbers and catalog values, what it could not read, and the first rows as they will be imported. You correct it in the conversation (“the Owner column is the IT owner”) or you approve.

![Once approved, the card shows the result of the import: six applications created, and the prepared file to download as .xlsx.](/screenshots/blog/plaid-import-result-2026-11-08.png)

**The list's own import, with your rights.** On approval, Plaid goes through the import of the list, with its checks and your rights: it imports what you could import yourself. The card shows what was created, updated or refused, with the line numbers of your file. The prepared file can be downloaded as `.xlsx`, and the audit log records each import. The AI model receives only a summary of the file.

**Simpler imports, with or without Plaid.** The main import dialogs offer **Download an example**: a workbook with sample rows and the accepted values. Columns carry their title in the screen language, and the import reads these titles in any language.

**Plaid knows the manual.** Plaid and the MCP server read the user manual of the installed version: to a “how do I…” question, Plaid answers with a link to the section. The details are in the [Plaid](https://doc.kanap.net/ai-assistant/) page of the manual.

## Business processes, the ISO 9001 way

![The business process list as a tree, with the Type column and the parent process.](/screenshots/blog/business-processes-tree-2026-11-08.png)

A process map is almost always hierarchical: macro-processes, processes, sub-processes. KANAP now describes it that way.

**A type and a parent.** Each process takes an ISO 9001 type (management, core or support) and a parent process. The list shows as a tree, pickers show the full path (Parent › Child), and search finds a process by the name of its type. Its page follows the other records, History tab included.

**Categories are gone.** They overlapped with the hierarchy and are removed without conversion: after the upgrade, existing processes have no type and no parent, ready for you to place them in the tree. If you host KANAP and want to keep your categories, export the process list before upgrading. See the [Business processes](https://doc.kanap.net/business-processes/) page of the manual.

## Say it once

**Less text on screen.** Help sentences that repeated the obvious have left every space, from budget to administration. Explanations that are still useful open from an information icon beside the label.

**Refusals that say why.** A refusal names the reason in the language of the interface: the missing right, a closed incident, a time entry of someone else. Plaid, the MCP server and the agents screens follow the same rule.

**A home page in sections.** The home page groups its tiles in sections (My work, Portfolio, IT landscape, Budget management), which you customize in place. A tile with nothing to show is hidden.

## Your workspace, your brand

**A logo for dark mode.** Branding accepts a second logo, shown in dark mode.

**Your workspace name.** The workspace name, set in **Administration > Branding**, appears in the browser tab, as the name of the installed app, in e-mail subjects and headers, and on the authorization page of AI tools connected through MCP. Without a logo, it takes the logo's place in the header and on the sign-in page. Password reset and invitation e-mails carry the workspace logo and colour. See the [Branding](https://doc.kanap.net/branding/) page of the manual.

## Other improvements

- **Limited data access**: people limited to part of the budget edit suppliers and contacts, as their role allows. Deleting and importing them stay reserved to people with access to all data.
- **An asset always has a location**, which you can create from the asset. A location that still holds assets can no longer be deleted.
- **The budget gets to the point.** The budget list opens on its amounts, a line's Budget tab on its chart, and the home Budget tile shows the total per year (OPEX and CAPEX on hover).
- **Password reset, account unlocked**: a completed reset lifts the block caused by failed sign-in attempts.
- **A deleted Plaid conversation is gone for good**, with its messages and attachments.
- **AI cost, answer by answer.** Each AI model can be given a reasoning effort, and **Usage & costs** adds up the cost of each Plaid answer.

This release also updates the dependencies of the API, the interface and the site, and fixes the open code scanning and Dependabot security alerts.

## On-premise

**The database role checked at every start.** Before the migrations, KANAP checks the PostgreSQL role of `DATABASE_URL`. A dedicated role that has `SUPERUSER` or `BYPASSRLS` loses these attributes first. A cluster administration role, such as `postgres`, stops the start-up before any migration. With a dedicated role, as the guide recommends, there is nothing to do.

**A start-up that stops cleanly.** On a refused role or a failed migration, the start-up stops at once, and the failed migration is rolled back: the database stays as it was. A lost database connection is still retried. Off a terminal, the API writes plain-text logs, with no colour codes.

A new installation also starts with Windows Server, Ubuntu and the starting portfolio classification. The trial, its e-mails and the discovery call belong to the hosted service. The upgrade follows the usual procedure.

## Upgrading

On the hosted service, there is nothing to do: you are already on 26.11.1.

If you host KANAP and follow the stable branch, go straight to 26.11.1, after reading the on-premise section of the [release notes](https://github.com/kanap-hq/KANAP/releases/tag/v26.11.1). If your installation predates 26.10.1, move it to `stable` first, as the [operations guide](https://doc.kanap.net/on-premise/operations/#upgrade-procedure) explains.
