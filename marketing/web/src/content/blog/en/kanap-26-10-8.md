The IT budget is negotiated in Excel. The financial controller sends you their workbook, management wants its tables in its own, and an auditor asks who changed a given envelope in June. Until now, KANAP spoke CSV, and the history of a record was read in the audit log, reserved for administrators.

26.10.8 builds the bridge. Every list and every report exports to Excel, every import accepts a workbook, and the budget file makes the round trip without conversion. Every record gains a **History** tab, readable by anyone who can open the record. And Plaid, the AI assistant built into KANAP, now covers every module, in read and, with your approval, in write.

## Excel, both ways

![The export dialog of a list offers CSV or Excel, with the number of rows that follow the list's filters.](/screenshots/blog/excel-export-menu-2026-10-28.png)

**Export what you see.** Every list exports to CSV or Excel, with the rows as they are filtered and sorted on screen. The workbook has the same columns as the list, and its numbers, amounts and dates are typed: no “12,000” as text, no date to convert back. Reports follow: budget reports, chargeback, time logged, capacity heatmap, end of support. The Excel file of a report adds a **Parameters** sheet that records the filters applied, so the recipient knows what they are looking at.

**Load a workbook as it is.** Every import accepts an `.xlsx` workbook alongside CSV, the budget file included. The first sheet is read, typed cells are taken exactly, and the report says if the workbook contains other sheets.

![Before loading a budget file, KANAP compares the workbook with what it holds: here, nothing to load, the 134 rows are unchanged.](/screenshots/blog/excel-budget-file-preflight-2026-10-28.png)

**The budget file checks before writing.** Before loading a budget file, KANAP compares it with what it holds: so many lines to create, so many to update, so many unchanged. When a column of the file replaces an amount built from calculation lines (quantity × price), the check names the lines, the columns and the years affected, with their total, before you decide.

**Every export leaves a trace.** The audit log records who exported what, with the format, the parameters and the number of rows. For budget data, knowing that a 2,000-row file went out on a Friday evening is not a detail.

The formats are detailed in the [CSV and Excel files](https://doc.kanap.net/csv-files/) page of the manual, and the budget file in [Load a budget from a spreadsheet](https://doc.kanap.net/budget-file/).

## Who changed what: the history of every record

![The History tab of a budget line lists the changes from the most recent to the oldest: author, date, field, old and new value.](/screenshots/blog/history-budget-line-2026-10-30.png)

Every record now has a **History** tab: budget lines, contracts, applications, servers, technologies, projects, requests, tasks, incidents, interfaces, companies, suppliers, cost centers, contacts, locations, documents. Records without tabs have a button.

**In words, not in JSON.** Each entry says who, when, and what changed, with the old and the new value: “Amount 2026, Budget column: 10,000 → 12,000”, “IT owner: Marc Petit → Sophie Laurent”, “Linked contract: SAP Maintenance added”. The yearly amounts of a budget line, the members of a project team, the links of an application appear in the history of the parent record. A change made by Plaid, by an import or by a synchronization states its source.

**Readable by anyone who can open the record.** No new right: if you read the line, you read its history. A value that points to a record of a module you do not read is shown without its name. The audit log in administration remains the complete source, with the raw values.

Plaid also answers “what changed on this line this month?”, with the same rights as the tab. The rules are in the [Record history](https://doc.kanap.net/record-history/) page of the manual.

## Plaid reads the whole IT estate, and writes under your control

![Plaid answers a question about the history of a budget line, with links to the records it cites.](/screenshots/blog/plaid-history-2026-10-30.png)

Since 26.10.1, KANAP has promised an assistant that knows your IT estate. This release keeps the promise module by module: we reviewed every screen and filled in what Plaid did not read.

**In read, everything.** Contracts and their deadlines, cost per FTE and average daily rate by supplier or cost center, budget by consolidation account, differences between two columns, FTE month by month, chargeback, incidents counted by application or by month with the average resolution time, technology versions and end of support, vulnerabilities as distinct CVEs, roles and data access for administrators, the history of a record, and the master data: cost centers, working-day calendars, analytics dimensions, exchange rates. OPEX and CAPEX lines form a single family: a question that does not specify covers both.

**In write, after a preview.** Plaid creates or changes an incident, adds a note to it, declares a technology on a server, changes the status of a vulnerability, deletes a task or a contact, after a preview that you approve. It does what the screen lets you do, with your rights, and nothing else. Previews and messages are in your language, with the labels of the screens.

**Frank about its limits.** When Plaid does not read something, it says so and names the screen that shows it, instead of answering “none”. Totals come from the server, never from an addition made by the model.

**Over MCP, the same answers.** Your own AI tool connected over MCP reads the same data, with the same rights, in read only. A refused call says why, and **Administration > Plaid** keeps the log of MCP calls, refused ones included. The details are in the [Plaid](https://doc.kanap.net/ai-assistant/) page of the manual.

## And every day

![A person without the right sees a page that names the missing right and offers to go back, inside the application frame.](/screenshots/blog/access-denied-2026-10-30.png)

- **A refusal that says why.** A page or an action outside your rights names the missing right and the module, in your language, and invites you to ask an administrator for it. Actions a reader cannot take are hidden or read-only.
- **The owners of a budget line, among the right people.** The IT and business owner pickers first offer the people who have access to the line's cost center, then the others.
- **A line that no longer matches its contract** (paying company, cost center, supplier, currency) carries a warning sign that states the differences.
- **The report by dimension** counts the lines of each value, details them, and separates OPEX and CAPEX.
- **Easier first steps**: the remaining trial days in the top bar, turning on Plaid from the home page, your name asked at first sign-in, and an **About KANAP** window with the version and access to support.
- **Dates you can type on the keyboard** without an intermediate save, and labels, numbers and messages in the interface language, down to the reports.

This release also updates the dependencies of the API, the interface and the site.

## If you host KANAP: ready-to-use images

From 26.10.8, your server no longer builds KANAP: it downloads the release images, published by our continuous integration to the GitHub registry, with verifiable provenance. The upgrade takes three commands: switch to the version, `pull`, `up -d`. Rolling back works the same way. A 4 GB server is enough, where the build needed 6.

This first upgrade requires one step on your side, described in the on-premise section of the [release notes](https://github.com/kanap-hq/KANAP/releases/tag/v26.10.8): open outbound access to the registry, then free the old build cache. On a large audit log, the migration that feeds the History tab takes a few minutes: plan the upgrade outside working hours.

## Upgrading

On the hosted service, there is nothing to do: you are already on 26.10.8.

If you host KANAP and follow the stable branch, go straight to 26.10.8, after reading the on-premise section of the [release notes](https://github.com/kanap-hq/KANAP/releases/tag/v26.10.8). If your installation predates 26.10.1, move it to `stable` first, as the [operations guide](https://doc.kanap.net/on-premise/operations/#upgrade-procedure) explains.
